Two operating models

Use your infrastructure—or use ours.

Both models can deliver the same governed intelligence. The difference is who owns the cloud and model contracts, who operates the environment, and how provider consumption is billed.

Enterprise default

Customer-owned infrastructure

Deploy DataKnobs inside the customer's approved cloud, data platform, model environment, IAM, and security boundary.

  • Customer pays cloud, data platform, and model providers directly
  • Best fit for deep integration and customer-controlled security boundaries
  • Preserves enterprise discounts, procurement relationships, and provider contracts
  • DataKnobs charges for platform intelligence plus activation and optional services
View customer-owned pricing
Turnkey option

DataKnobs-managed infrastructure

DataKnobs hosts and operates the environment while provider consumption stays inside an approved prepaid budget and operating policy.

  • DataKnobs manages hosting, monitoring, backups, and environment operations
  • Best fit when speed and reduced infrastructure overhead matter most
  • Provider consumption is metered separately from fixed platform and operations fees
  • Budget alerts, approval gates, forecasts, and hard-stop policies can be configured

Decision framework

Three factors usually dominate the choice.

1

Control & sensitivity

Customer-owned infrastructure is usually the cleanest fit when workloads contain regulated or highly confidential data, require strict residency, or must remain inside an existing enterprise security boundary.

2

Integration & operating maturity

Deep integration with enterprise warehouses, internal applications, private networking, IAM, and existing cloud operations generally favors customer-owned deployment.

3

Speed & operating burden

Managed infrastructure can reduce setup and day-to-day operations for focused workloads—provided the security, data-handling, and consumption profile can be explicitly governed.

Commercial model

Understand which costs are fixed, scoped, and variable.

The two deployment models share the same core platform economics, but managed infrastructure adds a fixed operations layer and a prepaid provider-consumption budget.

1

Activation

One-time discovery, integration, configuration, evaluation, security work, testing, and production launch.

2

Platform subscription

Annual DataKnobs / EKIP subscription based primarily on production Data Products and governed intelligence.

3

Environment operations

Customer-owned: operated by the customer. Managed: a separate fixed DataKnobs operations fee based on topology, support, and SLA.

4

Provider consumption

Customer-owned: paid directly to providers. Managed: prepaid, metered, and governed against an approved consumption budget.

FAQ

Common deployment questions.

Do both deployment models include the same core DataKnobs capabilities?

Yes. Both can include governed Data Products, Knobs, lineage, evaluation, monitoring, experimentation, optimization, and EKIP capabilities. The main difference is who owns and operates the infrastructure and provider contracts.

Is customer-owned infrastructure only for large companies?

No. It is the default when an organization needs its own security boundary, existing cloud contracts, deep internal integration, or direct control of provider consumption, regardless of company size.

Can DataKnobs-managed infrastructure support sensitive workloads?

Potentially, but only when the required security, privacy, contractual, residency, and compliance controls are explicitly scoped and approved. Customer-owned infrastructure remains the simpler default for many regulated or deeply integrated enterprise workloads.

Can we start managed and migrate later?

Yes. Portability can be designed into the architecture. Migration work, provider contracts, data movement, and security onboarding are scoped separately.

Are the prices on these pages final quotes?

No. Published figures are planning ranges and starting prices. Final commercial terms depend on Data Product scope, integrations, environments, support, security, compliance, and provider-consumption requirements.
