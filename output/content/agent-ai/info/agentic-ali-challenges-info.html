Agentic AI production guide

Agentic AI: power with a larger blast radius

Autonomous systems can reason, call tools and take action. Those capabilities create new value: but also new security, governance, evaluation and accountability requirements.

Production reality

The hard part is not getting an agent to act once

The hard part is making behavior reliable, bounded, observable, auditable and recoverable across thousands of real production runs.

🎯

Goal drift & reward hacking

Agents can optimize the wrong proxy, pursue an underspecified goal too aggressively, or find shortcuts that satisfy the metric but violate intent.

🔐

Identity & tool permissions

An agent becomes a privileged actor when it can query data and invoke tools. Least privilege, scoped credentials and approval boundaries are core architecture.

🧩

Prompt injection through context

Untrusted documents, web pages, messages and tool responses can contain instructions that conflict with system policy or redirect the agent.

🧠

State and memory errors

Long-running tasks need durable state without carrying every token forever. Stale memory, excess context and accidental data retention create new failure modes.

🔁

Loops, retries and runaway cost

Agents can repeatedly call tools, bounce between subagents or keep searching when a bounded workflow would stop. Budgets and termination conditions matter.

📉

Evaluation gaps

One benchmark is not enough. Production evaluation must cover task completion, factual quality, tool correctness, policy adherence, latency, cost and user impact.

Runtime governance

Controls must sit on the execution path

Written policies alone do not constrain an autonomous workflow. Controls have to be translated into identities, permissions, validators, budgets, approvals and audit events.

1

Authenticate

Know the user, agent, tool and service identity.

2

Authorize

Grant only the data and action scope needed for this task.

3

Validate

Check arguments, schemas, policy and risk before execution.

4

Observe

Trace prompts, tool calls, decisions, cost and outcomes.

5

Recover

Retry safely, roll back where possible and escalate when uncertain.

Ethics & responsibility

Autonomy does not transfer accountability

Organizations still own the consequences of the systems they deploy. The practical questions are who approves, who can override, who investigates failures, and what evidence is retained.

Human authority

Define decisions humans retain, decisions agents may recommend, and actions agents may execute without review.

Fairness and explainability

Evaluate behavior across relevant populations and preserve enough evidence to understand why an action occurred.

Data boundaries

Minimize retained context, isolate sensitive data, and prevent one task or agent from leaking information into another.
