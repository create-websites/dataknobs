1. Data Prep & Cleanup

Data ingestion and cleansing take the longest. Data is sourced from numerous locations (Snowflake, BigQuery, S3). Key steps involve filling gaps (imputation), fixing errors, formatting data, and eliminating redundancy. SQL, Python (Pandas), or Spark are typical tools for this.

2. Feature Engineering

Domain expertise shapes features, creating signals for the model. This includes crafting new columns from existing data—e.g., deriving 'weekday' from 'date' or a 'user's rolling purchase average'. Feature engineering is key to improved model results. The rise of Feature Stores Here are a few options, all of similar length to the original: * **SageMaker, Kubeflow** standardize feature access for training & predictions across the enterprise. * **Hopsworks, Michelangelo** empower organizations to share features seamlessly for training/serving. * **MLflow, Flink** assist companies in consistent feature delivery, training and production. * **Cortex, Determined** enable businesses to uniformly serve features across training and deployment.

3. Model Choice & Training

For tabular data, the undisputed champions are Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) Consider XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost: these GBDTs excel in accuracy, speed, and interpretability. Despite deep learning's presence, GBDTs often become the go-to solution. AutoML tools leverage all, often ensembling for top performance. Training happens on a 'training' set, validated on a separate set.

Understanding Tabular Models

Core to the process are the models. A "tabular model" uses algorithms to learn from table data. These models vary in effectiveness, and the field progresses quickly. Explore the options below.

Classical & Tree-Based Models

Here are a few options for rewriting the line, maintaining a similar size and meaning: * These models are fundamental to tabular ML and are heavily utilized. * The models are key for tabular ML, and widely employed in real-world scenarios. * Widely used in practice, these models are the core of tabular ML. * Tabular ML relies heavily on these models, the most commonly applied.

  • Linear/Logistic Regression: Here are a few options, all keeping a similar size and conveying the same core idea: * **Linear yet quick and clear, offering insight, though accuracy can lag.** * **Easy to grasp and swift, providing understanding, but less precise.** * **Simple, quick and insightful, but may sacrifice accuracy due to linearity.** * **Fast and understandable, yielding insights, though accuracy can be limited.** * **Interpretable and efficient, yet accuracy suffers due to linear models.**
  • Random Forests: A collection of decision trees, forming a strong, overfitting-resistant, and precise ensemble.
  • Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs): **Focusing on the models and their key feature:** Advanced models such as XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost use a sequential tree-building approach. Each new tree in the sequence refines the predictions of its predecessors. This iterative process is a key reason why they're so successful because:
    • Handle mixed data types (numbers and categories) natively.
    • Do not require feature scaling (e.g., normalization).
    • Are highly accurate and computationally efficient.

  • Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): A basic neural network's use demands careful data prep, including one-hot for categories & scaling for numbers.
  • Embeddings: A core advancement: instead of one-hot vectors, categorical inputs (e.g., 'product_id', 'zip_code') become low-dimensional embeddings, enabling the model to grasp category relationships.
  • Specialized Architectures: Models like TabNet and AutoInt Employing bespoke neural networks, akin to Transformers, to discern key features for predictions via attention mechanisms.

Major Challenges:

  • Heterogeneity: Tables are inherently disordered. Unlike continuous text, column arrangements lack intrinsic meaning, and dataset structures vary wildly. A 'age' column in a person's table holds a distinct meaning from 'age' within a building's records.
  • Data Access: Building a large, varied, and representative tabular data corpus for pre-training is exceptionally challenging, contrasting sharply with the readily available web text for LLMs.
