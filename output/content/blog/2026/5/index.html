The Month in a Sentence

May delivered a tale of two markets. A cohort of AI-leveraged, platform, and software names posted blistering double- and triple-digit growth, while pockets of hardware, life-science instruments, and discretionary retail wrestled with softer demand and a still-unsettled trade backdrop.

Across 724 earnings calls, the dominant storyline was less about a single macro shock and more about divergence: who is monetizing AI versus who is merely talking about it, who is passing through tariff and input costs versus who is absorbing them, and who is returning capital versus who is rebuilding. Below, the themes that recurred most, the standouts worth watching, and the pressure points to keep on the radar.

SECTION 4: TARIFFS
04

Tariff & Trade Watch

Trade policy named in roughly 1 in 10 calls :: concentrated, not universal.

Tariff exposure clustered predictably in industrials, materials, autos, and consumer hardware :: names such as AAON, Allison Transmission, Armstrong World, Asbury Automotive, ArcelorMittal, Adidas and Bath & Body Works featured in the discussion. The tone, however, has matured from alarm to management: companies are reciting mitigation playbooks :: sourcing shifts, selective price actions, and supply-chain regionalization.

Notably, for some the policy shift cut favorably. ArcelorMittal pointed to improved steel pricing and spreads in Europe as trade measures and regionalized market structures took hold :: a reminder that "tariff exposure" is not a one-directional risk.

SECTION 5: AI
05

AI: From Narrative to Numbers

The single most-cited theme of the season.

AI appeared in 42% of calls, but the texture changed. The clearest monetization stories came from the infrastructure layer :: AMD (data center +57%) and Arista (+35%, beating guidance) riding hyperscaler demand :: and from applied-AI leaders like Axon, whose AI product revenue grew over 700%. Elsewhere, AI showed up as an operating lever: companies from 1-800-Flowers (AI-driven product sorting) to platform businesses cited it as a margin and customer-experience tool rather than a standalone revenue line.

  • Infrastructure winners :: silicon, networking, and data-center names converting demand into revenue today.
  • Applied-AI leaders :: vertical software embedding AI into products with measurable attach and pricing power.
  • Operational adopters :: the long tail using AI to cut cost and lift conversion, where the payoff is in margins, not the top line.
SECTION 6: CAPITAL
06

Capital Returns & The Deal Pipeline

Balance-sheet confidence held up.

Nearly one in five companies addressed buybacks or dividends, and 31% touched M&A :: both signs that management teams, on balance, felt confident enough to deploy capital rather than purely conserve it. Roughly 10% referenced restructuring or cost-out programs, a measured level that points to disciplined trimming rather than broad retrenchment. Among the mega-caps, AbbVie exemplified the confident end of the spectrum: $15B in Q1 revenue (+12.4%), a raised full-year outlook (~$67.3B), and Skyrizi/Rinvoq momentum funding continued pipeline investment.

SECTION 7: TAKEAWAYS
07

What to Carry Forward

  • Monetization is the new dividing line. The market is separating companies booking AI revenue from those still narrating a roadmap.
  • Tariffs are managed, not feared. Exposure is concentrated and increasingly met with mitigation playbooks :: and occasionally turns into a tailwind.
  • Quality of growth matters. Several "growth" prints were carried by margins or one-offs; read past the headline to the core demand trend.
  • Capital deployment signals confidence. Steady buyback, dividend, and M&A commentary suggests boards aren't bracing for a downturn.
  • Watch the laggards' fixes. Life-science instruments, discretionary retail, and legacy-traffic businesses are mid-turnaround :: execution, not the macro, will decide them.
