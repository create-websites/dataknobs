Core Concepts

Optimal Transport (OT) offers a robust method for comparing distributions, identifying the most efficient transformation pathway. This section delves into OT's core concepts, charting its evolution from a specialized math problem to a data science staple.

Kantorovich's core advance in OT was the shift from Monge's rigid one-to-one mapping to a flexible one-to-many "transport plan," enabling mass splitting.

The OT Toolkit: Data-Centric Applications

Optimal Transport's power extends beyond theory, becoming a practical data science asset. Discover its applications in data generation, augmentation, alignment, and curation.

Generative Modeling (WGANs)

Employing the Wasserstein distance in WGANs stabilizes training, mitigating problems like mode collapse and thus producing superior synthetic data.

Data Augmentation

Wasserstein Barycenters offer a sound method for averaging data, such as images or embeddings, yielding realistic samples within the data's structure.

Domain Adaptation

By aligning a labeled source and unlabeled target domain, Optimal Transport (OT) enables generalization and mitigates domain shift.
