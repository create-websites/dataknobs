The 2026 roadmap: workflow first

The older “10-20-70” heuristic is useful as a reminder that organizational change matters, but it should not be treated as a universal formula. Current evidence points to the same conclusion more defensibly: Microsoft’s 2026 Work Trend Index found organizational factors such as culture, manager support and talent practices were associated with roughly twice the reported AI impact of individual factors in its model.

1 · Map the workflowDefine the outcome, current process, exceptions, owner and baseline.
2 · Bound authorityDecide what the agent may read, write, spend, send or change.
3 · Build a thin sliceUse representative cases and mock tools before broad integration.
4 · InstrumentTrace calls, tools, policy decisions, artifacts, cost and latency.
5 · EvaluateMeasure task success, process quality, safety and business value.
6 · Roll out graduallyStart assistive, then delegate bounded work as evidence improves.
7 · Learn as a systemFeed incidents, exceptions and wins into shared standards and regression tests.

Pilot selection matrix

High value + bounded authorityBest starting point. Clear outcome, reversible actions, good data and measurable baseline.
High value + broad authorityStrategic bet. Start with simulation, approvals and narrow scopes before delegation.
Low value + easyUseful for platform learning, but do not confuse technical success with business impact.
Low value + high riskDefer. Complexity and governance cost are unlikely to be justified.

Frequently asked questions

How should enterprises start with agentic AI?

Start with a measurable workflow and bounded authority. Define success and failure before selecting models or frameworks.

When should autonomy increase?

Increase autonomy only when representative evaluation and production evidence show the workflow is reliable enough for a larger action scope.
