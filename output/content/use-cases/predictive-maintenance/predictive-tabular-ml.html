The Tabular ML Ecosystem

Predictive tabular ML thrives within a diverse ecosystem of tools and platforms, spanning from basic open-source algorithms to advanced enterprise platforms that oversee the complete ML process.

Fundamental Tech & Libraries

These open-source libraries are essential for data scientists to construct high-performance models.

XGBoost

The supreme ruler of tabular data: a lightning-fast and precise Gradient Boosted Decision Tree (GBDT) library.

LightGBM

Microsoft's Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT) implementation is highly valued for its rapid training speed and effectiveness when working with large datasets.

CatBoost

Yandex's GBDT library is known for its automatic handling of categorical features, often leading to improved accuracy and simplified preprocessing steps.

AutoGluon-Tabular

Amazon has released an open-source AutoML library that automates model selection and hyperparameter tuning, resulting in powerful ensembles of multiple models.

TabNet

A Google-developed deep learning model tailored for tabular data that blends the interpretability of tree models with the strength of neural networks.

Scikit-learn

The essential Python library for machine learning, offering functionalities for data preprocessing, linear models, random forests, and beyond.

Enterprise Platforms

These platforms offer comprehensive solutions for creating, deploying, and overseeing ML models on a large scale.

Google Cloud Vertex AI

A consolidated platform providing AutoML for structured data, personalized training, feature repositories, and endpoints for model deployment.

AWS SageMaker

Amazon offers an extensive MLOps solution, which includes SageMaker Autopilot for tabular AutoML, notebook environments, and CI/CD pipelines

Azure Machine Learning

Microsoft's tool for ML lifecycle management includes a drag-and-drop designer, AutoML, and strong MLOps features.

Databricks

A company specializing in data and AI integration, offering a unified platform for data warehousing and AI, with robust support for Spark-based ML and AutoML.

H2O.ai & DataRobot

Cutting-edge platforms in the industry that are dedicated to 'Driverless AI' or automated machine learning, offering advanced automation and governance features.

1. Data Prep & Cleanup

This phase is highly time-consuming, as it involves extracting data from multiple sources such as Snowflake, BigQuery, and S3. Key tasks include dealing with missing values, fixing errors, standardizing formats, and eliminating duplicates, typically accomplished through SQL, Python (Pandas), or distributed frameworks like Spark.

2. Feature Engineering

Applying domain knowledge to generate signals for the model involves creating new features by extracting information from existing columns, like determining the 'day of the week' from a 'date', or calculating a 'user's 30-day average purchase value'. This is a crucial step for optimizing model performance. Feature Stores Feast and Tecton assist businesses in effectively managing, sharing, and delivering these capabilities consistently for training and live predictions.

3. Model Choice & Training

For tabular data, the undisputed champions are Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) Similar to XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost, these models are known for their high accuracy, speed, and ease of interpretation. Despite the existence of Deep Learning models, Gradient Boosting Decision Trees (GBDTs) are usually the preferred choice. Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) platforms will experiment with all these models and combine them to discover the most effective combination. The model is subsequently trained on a 'training' dataset and assessed on a 'validation' dataset.

4. Deployment

A model is only beneficial once it is deployed for use. Deployment can occur in two primary ways:
Batch Scoring: The model is scheduled to run regularly (e.g., nightly) to evaluate a significant amount of data (e.g., forecasting churn for all users), typically as a Spark job or SQL query.
Real-time Inference: The REST API-wrapped model is deployed on a server, such as Kubernetes or a platform like Vertex AI, enabling instant predictions for applications, like quickly determining whether to approve or deny a transaction.

5. Maintenance & Monitoring (MLOps)

Models degrade over time. Data drift occurs when the production data deviates from the training data, such as when a pandemic alters consumer buying habits. Model drift MLOps platforms such as MLflow, Arize, and Fiddler are utilized to detect drops in accuracy, monitor model drift, performance, and bias. Alerts are triggered when a model degrades, prompting a CI/CD pipeline to automatically retrain and deploy a new version of the model with updated data.

Understanding Tabular Models

The core of the lifecycle revolves around the models themselves, such as the 'tabular model' which is a machine learning algorithm specifically created to identify patterns within data presented in a table format. Each model possesses unique strengths and the field is constantly developing. Click on the tabs below to delve into the various categories.

Classical & Tree-Based Models

These models are the foundation of tabular machine learning and are commonly utilized in real-world applications.

  • Linear/Logistic Regression: Straightforward, quick, and easily understandable, but frequently less precise due to their limited ability to grasp complex relationships.
  • Random Forests: A collection of numerous decision trees that are robust, resistant to overfitting, and highly accurate.
  • Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs): Advanced models such as XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost construct trees in a sequential manner, with each new tree aiming to rectify the errors made by its predecessors. These models are considered dominant due to their:
    • Handle mixed data types (numbers and categories) natively.
    • Do not require feature scaling (e.g., normalization).
    • Are highly accurate and computationally efficient.

Deep Learning for Tabular Data

These models leverage neural networks to grasp intricate, non-linear patterns. While excelling in vision and language tasks, they are not as prominent in handling tabular data.

  • Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): Basic neural networks require meticulous preprocessing, including one-hot encoding for categories and scaling for numerical values.
  • Embeddings: A significant innovation is the use of embeddings to represent categorical features ('product_id' or 'zip_code') instead of one-hot encoding. This enables the model to comprehend the 'meaning' and relationships between various categories.
  • Specialized Architectures: Models like TabNet and AutoInt Custom neural networks are designed with attention mechanisms, similar to Transformers, to determine the most crucial features for a specific prediction.

The Concept of "Large Tabular Models" (LTMs)

This innovative research field is rapidly growing, driven by the achievements of advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT.

The Core Idea: Is it possible to pre-train a single, extensive 'foundation model' using a vast and varied assortment of tabular datasets, including public data from finance, biology, retail, and more?

This pre-trained long-term memory (LTM) is capable of learning universal, general patterns from tabular data. Subsequently, a company can adjust this model with their own unique dataset (such as for predicting customer churn) to achieve high accuracy with minimal data and effort.

Major Challenges:

  • Heterogeneity: Tabular data is disorderly, with columns lacking a set order and varying schemas across datasets. For example, the column 'age' in one table may represent something entirely different than in another table.
  • Data Access: Building a comprehensive, varied, and inclusive collection of public tabular data for pre-training is a challenging task, unlike the web-scraped text commonly used for LLMs.

Current Status: Primarily a theoretical idea, the prevailing approach remains focused on training a particular model (such as a GBDT) with a specific dataset to tackle a particular task.
